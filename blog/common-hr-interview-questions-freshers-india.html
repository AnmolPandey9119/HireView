25 Common HR Interview Questions & Answers for Freshers in India

Updated 29 July 2026 · 8 min read

If you're a fresher walking into your first HR round — whether it's a campus placement, a startup interview, or a government exam personal-interview round — the same handful of questions come up again and again. Knowing what's actually being tested behind each question matters more than memorizing a "perfect" answer. Here are 25 of the most common ones, what interviewers are really listening for, and how to structure a strong response.

1. Questions about you

Q: Tell me about yourself.
A: Keep it to 60-90 seconds. Structure: current status (degree/branch/college) → 1-2 relevant projects or experiences → why you're interested in this role. Don't repeat your resume line by line.
Why it's asked: it's rarely about the facts — it's testing whether you can communicate clearly and hold structure under pressure.
Q: What are your strengths?
A: Pick 1-2 strengths and immediately back each with a specific example — "I'm good at debugging under pressure, like when I fixed a production issue during my internship at 11pm before a demo."
Why it's asked: generic answers ("hardworking", "team player") without proof are an instant red flag for interviewers.
Q: What is your biggest weakness?
A: Name a real (not fake-humble) weakness, then show what you're actively doing about it. E.g. "I used to avoid public speaking, so I started presenting in my college tech club — still working on it."
Why it's asked: they're checking self-awareness and whether you can take feedback, not looking for a flaw-free answer.
Q: Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
A: Show a realistic growth path tied to the role you're applying for — deeper technical expertise, more ownership, maybe mentoring — not a vague "successful manager" answer.
Why it's asked: they want to know if you'll stick around and grow with the company, not job-hop in 6 months.

2. Questions about the role/company

Q: Why do you want to work here?
A: Mention something specific about the company (a product, a value, a recent achievement) — vague answers like "great company culture" signal you didn't research them.
Why it's asked: tests genuine interest versus a mass-applied, copy-paste application.
Q: Why should we hire you?
A: Connect 2-3 of your specific skills/projects directly to what the job description asks for. Be concrete, not "I'm a fast learner."
Why it's asked: forces you to make the case yourself instead of the interviewer guessing your fit.
Q: What do you know about our company?
A: Know their core product, roughly how big they are, and one recent news item (funding, launch, expansion). 2 minutes on their "About" page and LinkedIn is usually enough.
Why it's asked: basic prep signals you actually care about this specific opportunity.

3. Behavioral questions

Q: Tell me about a time you faced a conflict in a team.
A: Use the STAR method — Situation, Task, Action, Result. Focus more on your action and the resolution than on blaming the other person.
Why it's asked: real jobs involve disagreement — they're checking how you handle it, not whether you've never had one.
Q: Describe a failure and what you learned from it.
A: Pick a real failure with a clear lesson, not a disguised humble-brag ("I worked too hard"). End on what you'd do differently now.
Why it's asked: everyone fails — how you process it tells them how you'll handle setbacks on the job.
Q: How do you handle pressure or tight deadlines?
A: Give a concrete example of prioritizing tasks or asking for help under a real deadline, rather than a generic "I stay calm" claim.
Why it's asked: predicts how you'll behave during a genuinely stressful sprint or incident.

4. Salary & logistics

Q: What are your salary expectations?
A: For freshers, it's fine to say "I'm open to the standard package for this role at your company" or give a researched range — avoid naming a number far off from market rate without research.
Why it's asked: to check your expectations align with their budget before going further in the process.
Q: Are you willing to relocate / work in shifts?
A: Answer honestly — a mismatch here wastes everyone's time later. If genuinely flexible, say so clearly.
Why it's asked: logistics mismatches are one of the most common reasons offers fall through late in the process.

5. Closing questions

Q: Do you have any questions for us?
A: Always have 1-2 ready — about the team you'd join, what success looks like in the first 90 days, or growth opportunities. Never say "no, I think you covered everything."
Why it's asked: not asking anything reads as disinterest, no matter how good your earlier answers were.

A quick note on preparation

Memorizing exact scripts for all 25 of these tends to backfire — interviewers can tell when an answer is rehearsed word-for-word versus genuinely understood. A better approach is knowing the structure of a good answer for each category above, then practicing out loud until your real answers come naturally under a bit of pressure.
